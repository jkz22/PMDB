# PMDB v2 — learned representations for incoming-material QC: results

Scope: compare learned image representations of the Si/graphite anode SEM data, separated by detector
(BSE, Inlens, ETD/SE) and combined as the co-registered 3-channel stack, trained on the approved
baseline only and on all batches; audit the latent spaces for imaging/detector/field confounders and
rerun with those removed; and, in parallel, classify fields into Batch_1/2/3. All numbers below are
from `outputs/v2/results_tables.md` (auto-generated by `python -m src.v2.report` from the run
artefacts), `outputs/v2/leaderboard.csv`, `leaderboard_allkpi.csv`, `cls_leaderboard.csv`,
`cls_probe*.csv` and `audit/`. Every training run was a real CUDA job on an H100 (Modal); nothing fell
back to CPU.

## 1. Summary

1. **Best representation: VAE-C** (convolutional VAE with an auxiliary head predicting Kevin's gated
   KPIs), stack view, trained on the Batch_3 baseline only, on PR #16 `hybrid`-harmonised input
   (run `1a21dfaded41`). A ridge probe on its 128-d latent predicts the five gated KPIs on held-out
   fields with mean R² **0.844** (Si fraction 0.94, graphite 0.94, K01 0.94, pore 0.75, K04 agglomerate
   fraction 0.66), while predicting imaging statistics no better than chance (R² −0.03), identifying
   fields at 0.65× the chance-normalised rate and batch at 17% (chance 33%). It is the only family
   whose KPI signal did not depend on grey level: raw-input VAE-C scores 0.824, harmonised 0.844.
2. **Everything else is much weaker on microstructure.** Best per family (gated KPI R², held-out
   fields): MAE-adapted 0.62–0.72, DINOv2-FT 0.47–0.54, MAE-scratch 0.23–0.49, VAE-B 0.49 (collapses to
   ≈0 once grey level is harmonised), VAE-A 0.03–0.18, frozen DINOv2-S 0.42, ViT-MAE-B 0.46,
   NASA MicroNet EfficientNet-B4 0.26. The ViT/DINO families also carry the most imaging
   confounding (imaging-stat probe R² up to 0.94, field-ID 5–8× chance).
3. **Robustness of VAE-C.** Grouped 5-fold cross-fitting over all 31 fields: 0.883 ± 0.029 (all-batch
   aug0 config) and 0.837 ± 0.015 (baseline-only config). Two extra seeds: 0.809, 0.833. Leave-one-batch-out
   (train on two batches, probe KPIs on the unseen third): 0.76–0.80 raw, 0.76–0.83 hybrid, so the
   representation transfers to a batch it never saw.
4. **The Batch_3 grey-level artefact was real but is not what separates the batches.** PR #16's
   per-site LUTs remove the +7/+16/+5 black-level lift and 0.69× gain. Classifiers trained on
   harmonised images reach the same field accuracy as on raw images (ResNet-18/DINOv2/EffNet: 0.57–0.64
   either way; Batch_3 recall 0.76–0.94 either way). Imaging statistics alone (p1/p99/noise/sharpness)
   still classify 65% of fields, almost all of it Batch_3, which is also ~25% less sharp in BSE. That
   sharpness/noise difference is not removed by any grey-level LUT.
5. **Batch_1 vs Batch_2 is not separable with this data.** Kevin's KPIs classify the three batches at
   chance (field accuracy 0.36). Pairwise Batch_1-vs-Batch_2 (14 fields, leave-one-field-out): KPIs
   8/14, imaging statistics 5/14, median over 186 embedding probes 6/14; the best single probe reaches
   11/14, which is what the maximum of 186 chance-level probes looks like. Supervised classifiers get
   Batch_1 29–57% and Batch_2 14–43% of fields right (chance 33%). With 7 fields per batch the CIs are
   ±35 points; there is no evidence of a microstructural Batch_1/Batch_2 difference at 12.8 µm crop scale.
6. **Open question that images alone cannot settle:** whether Batch_3's black level and sharpness are
   microscope session settings or a real material/preparation effect. The microscope session logs
   (brightness/contrast, detector bias, working distance, focus) for the Batch_3 session, or one Batch_3
   sample re-imaged under the Batch_1/2 settings, would settle it.

## 2. Data, splits and training setup

- 31 aligned fields (Batch_1 7, Batch_2 7, Batch_3 17) from `cache/half` at 50 nm/px; channels
  `[BSE, Inlens, ETD|SE]`. Crops 256 px (12.8 µm); training stride 128 px with ±64 px random shift,
  evaluation stride 256 px (non-overlapping).
- **Views**: `stack` (3 channels as-is); `BSE`, `Inlens`, `SE_type` (one detector replicated to 3 channels).
- **Train sets**: `all` (26 fields) or `baseline` (Batch_3 fields only; Batch_3 is the approved pure
  batch — Section 6 compares the original Batch_1-baseline assumption). **Held-out screening fields**,
  never trained on in Stages A/B/R2/R3: `Batch_1/iv6g2oq0`, `Batch_2/i9jiqjwl`, `Batch_3/hawkfj64`,
  `Batch_3/hzumfsms`, `Batch_3/mgxahqnk` (221 crops). All metrics in the leaderboards are on these.
- **Inputs**: `raw` (grey/255), `norm` (teammate percentile normalisation), and the harmonisation
  options `gmm` (my earlier 3-peak per-image linear map, Round 2) and PR #16 `hybrid` / `affine2` /
  `histmatch` (materialised `cache/harmonised/<method>/half`, fixed grey/255, never percentile).
- **Augmentation**: `aug0` none; `aug1` shift + H/V flips (default); `aug2` = aug1 + simulated SEM
  nuisances (blur, gain 0.75–1.08, offset −0.04..+0.08, Poisson+Gaussian noise ≤0.012, ranges from the
  measured batch differences).
- **Families**: VAE-A (plain conv VAE, 128-d), VAE-B (KPI-conditioned), VAE-C (KPI auxiliary head),
  MAE-adapted (`facebook/vit-mae-base` fine-tuned at 256 px), MAE-scratch (ViT-S/16), DINO-FT (DINOv2-S,
  last 2 blocks, BYOL loss); frozen DINOv2-S, ViT-MAE-B, MicroNet EfficientNet-B4. AdamW, bf16, batch 64,
  cosine schedule; VAEs 4k steps, MAE-adapted 6k, MAE-scratch 8k, DINO-FT 4k.
- **KPIs**: Kevin's code at d23a116, unmodified. Gate-passing set used for training/selection:
  `frac_si`, `frac_graphite`, `frac_pore`, `K01_si_frac_adm`, `K04_agglom_frac`. K02/K03/K04 cluster
  density fail gates G2/G3 (Section 7) and are reported separately as an experimental track.
- **Selection metrics** (held-out fields, ridge/logistic probes, leave-one-field-out): `kpi_r2` (higher
  better); nuisance metrics, lower better: `img_r2` (imaging statistics), `image_id_ratio` (field
  identification vs chance), `lift_shift` (embedding movement under a simulated black-level lift),
  `recon_kpi_err` (VAEs only); plus `knn_batch_acc`. Selection score = rank average over these, with
  runs below 75% of the best `kpi_r2` ranked last (the nuisance metrics only break ties among
  representations that carry microstructure signal). Field-level results use crop means with bootstrap CIs.
- **Classification track**: 3-class Batch_1/2/3, stratified grouped 5-fold by field (each fold holds
  out 1–2 Batch_1, 1–2 Batch_2, 3–4 Batch_3 fields; no field in both train and test). Crop-level and
  field-level (majority vote) accuracy. Never leave-one-batch-out, since batch is the label.

## 3. Phase 0: data, imaging statistics, KPI gates

### Observed
- **Data.** There are 31 fields (Batch_1 7, Batch_2 7, Batch_3 17) and 124 TIFFs: 31 BSE, 31 Inlens, 27 ETD, 4 SE.
  - The TIFFs contain no microscope metadata, only resolution tags (25 nm/px), so detector and field come from the file names (`metadata/`).
  - Detector co-registration was checked by phase correlation on left, centre and right windows. The offset is at most 0.11 native px for every detector pair (`metadata/coregistration_summary.csv`).
- **Raw imaging statistics.** Computed per image and per evaluation crop (`imaging_stats/`).
  - Batch_3 has a raised black level (mean p1) on every detector, compared with Batch_1:

    | Detector | Batch_1 p1 | Batch_3 p1 |
    |---|---:|---:|
    | BSE | 0 | 7.2 |
    | Inlens | 1.7 | 15.9 |
    | SE/ETD | 0 | 4.7 |

  - Batch_3 also has lower normalised sharpness. For BSE it is 0.109 against 0.151 for Batch_1.
  - Batch_3 Inlens images have a larger dark mixture component: weight w0 = 0.39 against 0.22.
- **Teammate normalisation** (`pmdb.io.load_site`, p0.5→0, p99.5→1, clipped).
  - It is an exact affine map of the raw data: linear R² = 1 and lookup spread = 0, with gain = 1/(p99.5−p0.5).
  - Its parameters are set per image and per channel, not per batch or globally. Gain coefficient of variation: BSE 0.12, Inlens 0.09, SE/ETD 0.11.
- **KPI code.** Used from Kevin's `geometric-kpis` branch at d23a116c3d07, wrapped without modification (`src/v2/kpi_adapter.py`).
  - G1 synthetic phantom: passes. Si fraction is 4.85% against 4.87% truth, and D50 is 0.751 µm against 0.751 µm truth.
  - G2 1.2× upscale: fails for K03 D50. The ratio is 0.77–1.02 (mean 0.89), against the expected 1.20. Phase fractions move by at most 0.5 percentage points.
  - G3 native 25 nm vs 50 nm per field (n = 31). Values are half-res minus native, with the Spearman correlation between resolutions:

    | KPI | Mean relative difference | Spearman |
    |---|---:|---:|
    | Si fraction | −0.8% | 0.996 |
    | K01 | −0.8% | 0.994 |
    | K04 agglomerate fraction | +1.3% | 0.996 |
    | Pore fraction | −8.3% | 0.978 |
    | K02 | −20% | 0.974 |
    | K03 D50 | +26% | 0.924 |
    | K04 cluster density | −10% | 0.929 |

- **KPI set used** (decided with Jihan): `frac_si`, `frac_graphite`, `frac_pore`, `K01_si_frac_adm`, `K04_agglom_frac`.
  - K02, K03 and K04 cluster density are excluded until G2 passes.
  - K04 agglomerate fraction is undefined on 3.8% of evaluation crops and 4.2% of training crops, because those crops contain too few Si objects. These values are kept as NaN. Their loss is masked in VAE-C. In VAE-B they are passed as a missing-value indicator.

### Interpretation
- The Batch_3 black-level and sharpness differences are imaging differences between batches. No metadata field records the cause, so nothing causal is claimed.
- Batch identity is therefore partly confounded with imaging condition for any representation trained on raw counts. The latent audit in Phase 6 tests this directly.
- Size and count KPIs change with resolution and do not scale under a pure geometric enlargement. This is most likely because the segmentation uses smoothing and minimum-area steps defined in fixed pixels; that explanation is not verified.

## 6. Baseline choice: Batch_3 vs Batch_1

The original code assumed Batch_1 was the baseline; Jihan corrected this to Batch_3 (pure batch) and
the baseline-only runs were retrained on 14 Batch_3 fields (vs 6 Batch_1 fields). VAE-C: 0.801 (Batch_1
baseline) → 0.812–0.844 (Batch_3). The novelty/conformal scores in `predict.py` and the audit's field
inference are against Batch_3. Baseline-only training costs ≤0.03 KPI R² vs all-batch training for
VAE-C and is what a deployed "compare to approved batch" model would use.

## 7. Experimental all-KPI track (K02, K03, K04 cluster density; fail gates G2/G3)

Trained and scored separately (`leaderboard_allkpi.csv`, 24 extra runs on a second GPU). K03 particle
size is undefined on 85% of 12.8 µm crops (too few Si particles) and could not be scored on held-out
crops at all. Best: VAE-C stack/all/aug1 conditioned on all 10 KPIs — R² 0.734 over the 10 KPIs, 0.487
over the ungated ones (K02 count density, K04 cluster density), vs 0.15–0.23 on the ungated KPIs for
gated-trained models; its gated-KPI R² (0.833) is unchanged. These numbers are flagged experimental:
the ungated KPIs change 10–26% with pixel size and do not scale under a 1.2× enlargement, so a model
reading them is partly reading resolution.

## 9. Recommended model and `predict.py`

Recommended representation: **VAE-C, stack, Batch_3 baseline, aug1, PR #16 `hybrid` input, run
`1a21dfaded41`** (checkpoint `outputs/v2/runs/1a21dfaded41/final.pt`). Rationale: highest KPI R²,
lowest field-ID and batch leakage, stable under CV / seeds / LOBO, and its KPI signal survives
harmonisation (unlike VAE-B). A deployed pipeline should apply the hybrid LUT to each new field before
embedding (predict.py does this by re-fitting the field's LUT from its own BSE anchors, byte-identical
to PR #16's materialised cache for known sites).

`python predict.py --sites Batch_3/hawkfj64 Batch_1/iv6g2oq0 --npz cache_heldout/half/*.npz --representation 1a21dfaded41`
on the three unlabelled held-out sites plus two labelled fields:

| field | KPI verdict (|z| > 3 vs Batch_3 field KPIs) | representation novelty (conformal p vs Batch_3 fields) |
|---|---|---|
| Batch_3/hawkfj64 | PASS (all 5) | PASS, p = 0.83 |
| Batch_1/iv6g2oq0 | REVIEW: pore fraction 0.053 vs 0.038 ± 0.004 (z = 3.8) | REVIEW, p = 0.056 |
| heldout 3e122cbj | **REVIEW: Si fraction 0.137 vs 0.061 ± 0.010 (z = 7.7); graphite 0.786 (z = −10.9); K01 z = 7.8** | PASS, p = 0.89 |
| heldout fn0mhxef | PASS | PASS, p = 0.83 |
| heldout xrv9xvzb | PASS | PASS, p = 0.61 |

The KPI path flags `3e122cbj` as having more than twice the baseline Si fraction; the field-mean
embedding-distance path does not, which shows that a whole-field mean of a 128-d latent is a blunt
novelty detector even though the latent encodes Si fraction at R² 0.94. The KPI verdict and the
VAE-C KPI head are the right tools for "is the microstructure different"; the latent distance is a
coarse "is anything different" check. `--representation best` resolves to leaderboard rank 1.

## 10. Limitations and what would settle the open questions

- 31 fields, 7 per minority batch: field-level accuracies have ±35-point CIs; the Batch_1/Batch_2
  conclusion is "no evidence", not "no difference".
- Imaging vs material for Batch_3: needs microscope session metadata or a re-imaged Batch_3 sample
  under Batch_1/2 conditions. `Site.raw_stats` (PR #16) keeps the pre-harmonisation statistics if the
  artefact itself is wanted as a QC feature.
- K02/K03/K04-density depend on pixel size at d23a116; any model trained on them is partly reading
  resolution. Re-run the gates when the segmentation uses physical units.
- `lift_shift` is not comparable between raw and harmonised runs (Section 4 footnote).
- Field-mean novelty is insensitive (Section 9); a KPI-head or crop-level conformal score would be the
  next step.
- Only 256-px crops at 50 nm/px were used; larger context (full-field or 25 nm/px) was out of scope.
